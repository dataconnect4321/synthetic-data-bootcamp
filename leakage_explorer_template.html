How this works

Each point is made of actual synthetic tables: the AIM hybrid refitted with repair amount τ from a fold's saved private measurements (ε 3, δ 1e-5); masking and consistency rules stay on. Refits are post-processing, so every τ has the same privacy. Plain AIM (left of the τ axis) is the same measurements with no masking, repair or rules: the privacy-only baseline. A point is the mean over refits within each fold, then over folds; the band is ± 1 sd across the fold means, the convention of the ± in RESULTS.md. All metrics are from run_adult_flip.py --score-csv (scripts/run_tau_grid.sh, results/utility_tau_grid.csv). Before vs after shows one release (fold 0, refit 0), so its numbers differ slightly from these means.

The strip at the top compares plain AIM with the hybrid at τ 1 in each fold (same measurements, so the difference is paired): Δ is the mean of the per-fold differences, ± their sd, and the count says in how many folds the hybrid moved the same way.

FLIP (square): the values the FLIP paper reports for Adult at λ 4, ε 3 (its Fig. 3), computed by its own evaluation code, so not the same way as the AIM points (e.g. its income AUC is 0.66; our scorer gives 0.90 on our reproduction of FLIP). FLIP does not report validity or impossible rows (n/r). Hover a FLIP mark for our reproduction (fold 0, scored like AIM; results/final_comparison.csv). FLIP's ε is nominal and covers the VAE only; AIM's ε covers the whole release.

BER: balanced error of a classifier predicting the table's own sex column from its other columns (0.5 = no link). A-NCB: the FLIP paper's cluster-balance score with its own-sex adversary (higher = groups mix in the clusters). It jumps once the sex label stops being predictable, so it mostly repeats BER; RESULTS.md leaves it out of the verdict. Income AUC: an income classifier trained on the table, tested on real held-out people. Parity / EO gap: that classifier's gap between real men and women in >50K predictions (all / true earners). Validity: share of rows passing rules learned from the real data. Impossible rows: rows with a sex–category pair absent from real data (e.g. a male wife).

How this works

The sliders drive a simulation in the browser, not a new release: each [ × column] count table gets Gaussian noise with an equal share of the zCDP budget (δ 1e-5), then each group's distribution is moved τ of the way to the pooled one with the transport plans of aim_fair/fair_aim.py. The column's overall distribution stays the same, so τ costs no distortion. It covers single columns only (no model fit, masking or rules), and real AIM's noise is larger. The buttons AIM plain / hybrid jump to ε 3 and overlay the actual releases (dashed).

The page holds exact counts of the real training fold: it is not a DP release.

How this works

AIM first measures every column's 1-way counts, then in each round picks the 2-way table its current model gets most wrong (exponential mechanism) and measures it with Gaussian noise. Private-PGM fits its graphical model to exactly these tables, so a pair that was never measured is only linked through other columns. This is one saved run (fold 0), read from its noisy measurements (aim_fair/run_aim.py); the sliders above do not change it. Node size: number of levels or bins. Pairs with are where the release can learn 's link to other columns; the hybrid's repair moves these tables before the final fit.
