How this works

Each point is made of actual synthetic tables: the AIM hybrid refitted with repair amount τ from a fold's saved private measurements (ε 3, δ 1e-5); masking and consistency rules stay on. Refits are post-processing, so every τ has the same privacy. Plain AIM (left of the τ axis) is the same measurements with no masking, repair or rules: the privacy-only baseline. A point is the mean over refits within each fold, then over folds; the band is ± 1 sd across the fold means, the convention of the ± in RESULTS.md. Fold 0 shows that fold alone (the mean of its 3 refits, no band): at τ 1 these are the same tables, and so the same numbers, as the AIM column of the FLIP-faithful tab. BER, A-NCB, AUC, validity and impossible rows are from run_adult_flip.py --score-csv; the downstream fairness metrics follow FLIP's own protocol (aim_fair/flip_protocol.py, LightGBM). The grid: scripts/run_tau_grid.sh, results/utility_tau_grid.csv. Before vs after shows one release (fold 0, refit 0), so its numbers differ slightly from these means.

The strip at the top compares plain AIM with the hybrid at τ 1 in each fold (same measurements, so the difference is paired): Δ is the mean of the per-fold differences, ± their sd, and the count says in how many folds the hybrid moved the same way.

FLIP (square): the values the FLIP paper reports for Adult at λ 4, ε 3 (its Fig. 3), computed by its own evaluation code, so not the same way as the AIM points (e.g. its income AUC is 0.66; our scorer gives 0.90 on our reproduction of FLIP). FLIP does not report validity, impossible rows or its per-target means (n/r). Hover a FLIP mark for our reproduction (fold 0, scored like AIM; results/flip_faithful_comparison.csv). FLIP's ε is nominal and covers the VAE only; AIM's ε covers the whole release.

BER: balanced error of a classifier predicting the table's own sex column from its other columns (0.5 = no link). A-NCB: the FLIP paper's cluster-balance score with its own-sex adversary (higher = groups mix in the clusters). It jumps once the sex label stops being predictable, so it mostly repeats BER; RESULTS.md leaves it out of the verdict. Income AUC: an income classifier trained on the table, tested on real held-out people. Parity / EO gap: FLIP's protocol, a LightGBM income model trained on the table and tested on real held-out people: its gap between men and women in >50K predictions at threshold 0.5 (all / true earners, the TPR gap; FLIP does not define its EO number). Eq. 14 / Eq. 15: the same, with every other attribute as the target in turn: the largest gap over classes in the share predicted (categorical), and the distance between the sexes' predicted values over the real sd (numeric), averaged over targets. Validity: share of rows passing rules learned from the real data. Impossible rows: rows with a sex–category pair absent from real data (e.g. a male wife).

Other datasets (ACS-I, Dutch): only τ 1 and plain AIM were run (aim_fair/run_datasets.sh, results/datasets_summary.csv), so there is no τ curve and no FLIP mark (the FLIP paper reports no results for them, and our FLIP reproduction covers Adult only). The scorecard asks four questions instead: sex guessed right = 1 − BER (50% = a coin flip, ✓ at 52% or less); the outcome's parity gap and the mean Eq. 14 gap over the category attributes (✓ if smaller than plain AIM's); the cost in AUC (✓ if under 0.03). The downstream numbers follow FLIP's protocol (aim_fair/flip_protocol.py --dataset D), as on Adult, but only their means were saved, so they have no ± sd; the strip's Δ is the difference of the fold means, not paired. Dutch's target is occupation, so its AUC and gaps are about occupation, and it has no numeric columns (no Eq. 15). Without their fold-0 data on disk only this tab is shown.

Which attributes got fairer: FLIP's protocol attribute by attribute: each attribute in turn is predicted by a LightGBM model trained on the table and tested on real held-out people, and the bar is the gap between the sexes' predictions (Eq. 14 for category attributes, W1 / real sd for number attributes). Coded column names are shown in words (e.g. SCHL = education); the table has the codes and the real data's gaps. Lower means the table teaches less of a sex difference about that attribute. Mean of 3 refits × 3 folds; the hybrid is τ 1 (for Adult, the τ grid's τ-1 tables). From results/flip_protocol_targets*.csv.

How this works

The sliders drive a simulation in the browser, not a new release: each [ × column] count table gets Gaussian noise with an equal share of the zCDP budget (δ 1e-5), then each group's distribution is moved τ of the way to the pooled one with the transport plans of aim_fair/fair_aim.py. The column's overall distribution stays the same, so τ costs no distortion. It covers single columns only (no model fit, masking or rules), and real AIM's noise is larger. The buttons AIM plain / hybrid jump to ε 3 and overlay the actual releases (dashed).

How this works

AIM first measures every column's 1-way counts, then in each round picks the 2-way table its current model gets most wrong (exponential mechanism) and measures it with Gaussian noise. Private-PGM fits its graphical model to exactly these tables, so a pair that was never measured is only linked through other columns. This is one saved run (fold 0), read from its noisy measurements (aim_fair/run_aim.py); the sliders above do not change it. Node size: number of levels or bins. Pairs with are where the release can learn 's link to other columns; the hybrid's repair moves these tables before the final fit.
